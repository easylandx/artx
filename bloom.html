The Piece

A shoreline that answers when it's touched

The Hour the Lanterns Bloom is a browser-based light piece: twelve procedural lanterns suspended over a bed of generated tideglass water. Each lantern is dormant until a visitor interacts with it, at which point it blooms — brightening, casting a reflection into the water below, and ringing a single soft tone tuned to its position in the field.

Fenmoor built the piece as a study in held light — the idea that a digital artwork can wait, patiently, for a viewer's hand before it becomes whole. No two visits bloom the same lanterns in the same order, and the piece never resets itself; it only fades when you ask it to.

The work is part of the artist's ongoing series exploring tide pools, glasswork, and the hour just after dusk when coastal towns light their first lamps — a recurring setting across Fenmoor's practice since her early sketchbooks.

I wanted a piece that could only be finished by someone else's hand. The lanterns aren't lit for you — they're lit by you.
— Talia Fenmoor, artist statement

News from the shoreline

This Week

"The Hour the Lanterns Bloom" opens to the public

Talia Fenmoor's newest interactive piece went live this week, inviting visitors to individually wake each of its twelve lanterns. Early viewers report spending far longer with the work than expected — several returning after dark specifically to bloom the field a second time.

Tideglass Quarterly

What is the Tideglass School, really?

A explainer on the loosely organized movement Fenmoor is associated with: artists building slow, generative pieces around water, glass, and lamplight, united less by a manifesto than by a shared hour of the day — dusk, and the first lamps of a coastal town.

Studio Notes

Fenmoor on sound design: "one tone per lantern"

In a short studio note, Fenmoor describes tuning each lantern's chime individually so the field, once fully bloomed, plays a complete — if unpredictable — melody, different every time depending on the order visitors choose.

Upcoming

A companion piece is in progress

Fenmoor has hinted at a second work continuing the series, reportedly set at low tide rather than dusk. No release date has been confirmed.
