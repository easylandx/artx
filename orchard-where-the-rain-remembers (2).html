Five Studies for an Orchard

Fenmoor renders the installation's source drawings only in line — no photograph accompanies the piece. Each plate is a single uninterrupted stroke, the way she says memory moves: without lifting the pen.

the artist

Talia Fenmoor

Fenmoor grew up beside her grandmother's orchard, in a valley where storms arrived on a near-yearly rhythm strong enough to be dated by. She has spent a decade making work that treats weather as an archive — something that falls, pools, and is absorbed into whatever it touches.

Orchard Where the Rain Remembers began as ninety line drawings made during a single wet November, later etched into copper wire and suspended above basins of collected rainwater. Motion sensors trigger recordings of family voices whenever a visitor passes beneath a branch — the piece listens as much as it is looked at.

Fenmoor's past installations have shown in Lisbon, Oaxaca, and a decommissioned granary outside Kraków. This is her first orchard built indoors.
