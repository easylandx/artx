The Piece

01 / Provenance

Oyelaran built Casimir from the salvaged ribs of forty-one broken umbrellas, straightened, annealed, and re-forged into a single radiating armature. Each rib was hand-filed to carry a different notch pattern — a private cipher the artist calls the piece's "weather alphabet."

The result behaves less like a sculpture than an instrument left mid-reading. Visitors report the ribs feel faintly warm near a window on overcast days, and the etched slate base has been observed to hold condensation in patterns that don't quite match the room's humidity.

It belongs to Ember Atlas Ornate — a movement Oyelaran co-founded in 2021, defined by radial, cartographic linework layered over domestic objects, rendered in fire-toned metals and read like instruments of divination rather than décor.

Dispatches

03 / Press & Notes
Ember Atlas Ornate gains a second signature work as Casimir enters private collection
+
The piece left Oyelaran's Lagos studio for a private collection in Madrid, its first change of hands since completion. Conservators noted the slate base had begun to hold a faint permanent condensation ring.
Critics debate whether the "weather alphabet" notches encode a real cipher
+
A cataloguer attempted to map the forty-one rib notches to a phonetic system; Oyelaran has neither confirmed nor denied a working cipher exists.
Oyelaran on Ember Atlas Ornate: "furniture that keeps a diary of the room"
+
In a rare interview, the artist described the movement's founding idea — objects that log ambient change through material rather than mechanism.
Visitors report the ribs feel warm before rain — conservators remain unconvinced
+
The studio has not disputed the reports, only noted that brass is, unremarkably, a good conductor of ambient temperature shifts.
